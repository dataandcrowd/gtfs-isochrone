Origin-destination commuting flows were taken from the 2023 Census "main
means of travel to work by Statistical Area 2" table (Stats NZ table
121988). This dataset records the number of commuters by travel mode for
every residence-SA2 to workplace-SA2 pair. We restrict to the 619
Auckland SA2s, yielding 28,081 intra-regional OD pairs. Car-based
commuters are defined as the sum of private-car drivers, company-car
drivers, and car passengers, totalling 297,762 commuters. Suppressed
cells (coded -999 by Stats NZ for confidentiality) are treated as zero.

REPLACE WITH:
The equity analysis proceeds in three stages. First, the Concentration
Index is applied to the baseline Auckland accessibility surface at the
SA2 level. Second, for each pricing scenario, every origin-destination
commute pair is classified by whether it crosses the priced zone and
whether the commuter has a viable public-transit alternative; burden is
then aggregated as a count of car commuters rather than a count of SA2
units. Third, spatial autocorrelation diagnostics (Global Moran's I and
Local Moran's I) are computed to test whether the trapped-payer burden
clusters geographically.

Stage 2: OD-based burden classification

To classify every commute under each scenario, we first identify which
OD pairs are charged. For the three CBD cordon scenarios (1a, 1c, 2c),
an OD pair is charged when the workplace SA2 lies inside the cordon
boundary and the residence SA2 lies outside it — that is, the commuter
drives inward across the cordon to reach work. For the three motorway
corridor scenarios (3b, 3c, 3e), an OD pair is charged when the
straight-line desire path between the residence and workplace SA2
population-weighted centroids intersects the dissolved motorway-corridor
footprint. This is an approximation of a route-based test; an exact
shortest-path test would require modelled car routes, which are outside
the current pipeline.

A commute is judged to have a viable public-transit alternative when the
residence-to-workplace trip can be completed by transit within 45 minutes
during the 07:00–09:00 peak, as recorded in the r5py travel-time matrix
(Section 2.3). A charged commuter whose trip has no transit option within
this bound is classified as a "trapped payer". Of all Auckland car
commuters, only 25.1% have a transit alternative within 45 minutes for
their specific commute, and just 9.9% within 30 minutes.

Based on these two conditions, each OD pair is assigned one of three
burden classes, and burden is aggregated as a weighted count of car
commuters:

  - Pays with alternative: the commute crosses the priced zone AND is
    feasible by transit within 45 minutes. These commuters bear the
    charge but have a realistic option to switch mode.

  - Pays without alternative (trapped payer): the commute crosses the
    priced zone BUT no transit option exists within 45 minutes. These
    commuters face the charge with limited ability to avoid it. This is
    the group of greatest equity concern.

  - Does not pay: the commute does not cross the priced zone.
    Regardless of transit access, the commuter is not directly exposed.

For each scenario, the trapped-payer count per SA2 of residence was
cross-tabulated against the NZDep2023 decile, and a Concentration Index
was computed using the trapped-payer commuter count as the outcome
variable. This enables direct comparison across all six scenarios and the
Step 1 baseline.


Stage 3: Spatial autocorrelation

To test whether the trapped-payer burden clusters geographically — a
question the Concentration Index cannot answer, since it measures
association with deprivation rank rather than spatial contiguity — we
computed Global Moran's I and Local Moran's I (LISA) statistics. Queen
contiguity weights were constructed from the 619 Auckland SA2 polygons
(1 connected component, no islands). Global Moran's I was computed for
the baseline accessibility surface (access_45min) and for the
trapped-commuter count under each scenario. Local Moran's I identifies
individual SA2s that form statistically significant (p < 0.05) spatial
clusters: High-High (HH, hotspots), Low-Low (LL, coldspots), and
spatial outliers (HL, LH). Pseudo-significance was assessed with 999
conditional random permutations (seed = 42 for reproducibility).

3.2. Scenario burden classification

Under the six shortlisted scenarios, the burden distribution — now
measured as the number of car commuters affected rather than the number
of SA2 units — reveals a stark picture of transit inadequacy (Table 1).
The CBD inner-cordon scenarios 1a and 1c charge 19,830 and 38,934
commuters respectively (6.7% and 13.1% of all Auckland car commuters).
The expanded Isthmus double cordon 2c charges 50,745 (17.0%). The
motorway scenarios affect far more commuters: 3b charges 223,644
(75.1%), 3c charges 228,063 (76.6%), and 3e charges 58,053 (19.5%).

The most striking finding is the proportion of charged commuters who
are trapped — lacking any transit alternative within 45 minutes for
their specific commute. Across all six scenarios, between 73% and 93%
of charged commuters are trapped payers. The Isthmus double cordon 2c
is the worst: 93.3% of its charged commuters have no viable transit
option. Even the smaller CBD cordons 1a and 1c, which charge
comparatively few commuters, trap 73–75% of those they do charge.

with a transit alternative defined as residence-to-workplace transit
time ≤ 45 min (07:00–09:00 peak).

1a       |    19,830  |     6.7%  |    5,286  |   14,544  |    73.3%
1c       |    38,934  |    13.1%  |    9,819  |   29,115  |    74.8%

Scenario-level Concentration Indices

The CI of the trapped-payer commuter count against NZDep2023 deprivation
rank is reported in Table 2. The CBD cordon scenarios (1a, 1c) show
negative CI values of -0.129 and -0.123, indicating that the trapped
burden falls disproportionately on wealthier areas — a progressive
pattern. The expanded cordon 2c is the most regressive scenario, with
CI = +0.050, though the magnitude is modest. The motorway scenarios 3b
and 3c show weakly positive CI values of +0.029 and +0.025
respectively. Scenario 3e is near-neutral at -0.031.

A notable departure from the SA2-count-based analysis is that the CI
values are uniformly close to zero. This does not mean the burden is
equitably distributed in absolute terms — it means that the trapped
condition is so widespread (73–93% of charged commuters) that it
affects wealthy and deprived areas alike, leaving little room for
distributional skew.

Spatial autocorrelation

Global Moran's I for the baseline accessibility surface is 0.852
(p = 0.001), confirming strong spatial clustering: high-access SA2s
concentrate around the CBD, while low-access SA2s dominate the
periphery. For the trapped-commuter counts, Global Moran's I ranges
from 0.38 (scenario 3b) to 0.61 (scenarios 2c, 3e), all significant
at p = 0.001. This means that despite the near-zero CI values — which
indicate no systematic association with deprivation — the trapped
burden is strongly spatially clustered.

Local Moran's I (LISA) identifies the specific clusters. For the
motorway scenario 3c, 59 SA2s form trapped-payer hotspots (HH) and 86
form coldspots (LL). The hotspot SA2s fall into two geographically
distinct groups: (1) the north-western new suburbs (Hobsonville Point,
Royal Heights), which are low-to-moderate deprivation, car-dependent
developments with no rail access; and (2) southern Auckland (Mangere,
Papatoetoe, Favona, Rosehill), which are high-deprivation areas with
limited transit coverage. The fact that hotspots span both affluent
and deprived areas explains why CI is near zero despite strong spatial
clustering — the deprivation effect cancels out.

Hypothesis evaluation

H1, that CBD cordon scenarios (1a, 1c, 2c) are broadly progressive,
is supported for the inner cordons: under 1a and 1c the trapped burden
falls on wealthier areas (CI = -0.13 and -0.12). The expanded cordon
2c is a partial exception: it is the only scenario with a clearly
positive CI (+0.050), though the magnitude is small. At the same time,
2c has the highest trapped rate (93.3%), making it the most problematic
scenario overall — nearly all charged commuters have no transit
alternative, and the modest regressive tilt compounds this.

H2, that motorway corridor scenarios (3b, 3c, 3e) are broadly
regressive, receives only weak support. The CI values (+0.025 to
+0.029 for 3b and 3c) are positive but far smaller than would be
expected if the burden were strongly concentrated in deprived areas.
The more salient finding is the sheer scale of the trapped population:
approximately 180,000 commuters under 3b and 3c, comprising roughly
80% of all charged commuters, lack a viable transit alternative. The
spatial analysis reveals that this burden clusters geographically
(Moran's I = 0.38–0.61) but not systematically by deprivation (CI
near zero), because the car-dependent suburbs that generate trapped
payers span the full socioeconomic spectrum.

The overarching conclusion is that the equity challenge posed by
congestion pricing in Auckland is less a matter of regressive
distribution across income groups and more a matter of near-universal
transit inadequacy. With only 25% of car commuters having a feasible
transit alternative within 45 minutes, any pricing scenario will trap
the majority of those it charges — regardless of deprivation level.
This shifts the policy implication from targeted fare concessions
toward investment in public-transport infrastructure, particularly in
the car-dependent new suburbs and outer corridors where trapped-payer
hotspots concentrate.
